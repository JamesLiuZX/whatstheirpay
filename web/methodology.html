Methodology v2026.10

How an estimate is made

Every estimate is calculated, not guessed: the same inputs always give the same answer, and the card shows each factor that moved the number. Nothing here uses anyone's real payroll data.

The formula

Base = role benchmark × level × employer type × market × experience.

Bonus or commission = base × the role's target variable pay, which rises 18% per level above mid for bonus roles and is scaled by employer type. Sales roles use commission at roughly constant on-target splits.

Equity = base × the employer type's typical equity share × the role's equity intensity × the level's equity multiplier. Private-company equity is labeled as paper value.

Components under 2% of base are dropped rather than shown as noise.

Role benchmarks

Titles are matched to 45+ role families (Software Engineering, Product Management, Nursing, Investment Banking…). Each family has a benchmark US base salary for a mid-level individual contributor at a typical company, set from public sources such as government wage statistics, H-1B salary disclosures and pay ranges in job posts.

Market

About 30 US metros and 50+ countries, each with a pay multiplier against the US national level, converted to local currency at approximate exchange rates (shown with a USD reference). In markets where global employers pay far above local ones (India, Brazil, the Philippines…), global employer types get a market premium and IT-services firms a discount. Some families, such as consulting and banking, follow national pay scales more than local ones.

Experience

Years of experience nudge pay within a level: about ±1.2% per year away from the level's typical tenure, capped between −4.8% and +7.2%.

It is capped at 95 (lower for inherently noisy groups such as founders and students). The range width follows from it: with spread s = 0.08 + (1 − confidence/100) × 0.25, the likely range is median × (1 − s) to median × (1 + 1.15s). Pay distributions skew right, so the upper side is wider. Pro adds the p10–p90 tails.

Job posts

When a job post shows a salary range (required in a growing number of places), we annualize it and compare its midpoint with the market's median base for that role, flagging posts more than 5% above or below.

Limits of the model

Estimates describe the market for a role, not a person. They can't see individual negotiation, performance, refreshers, or company-specific bands, and private-company equity values are illiquid. When we know less, the range gets wider instead of pretending to precision.
